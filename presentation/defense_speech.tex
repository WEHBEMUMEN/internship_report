3. \textbf{Hyper-Reduction via ECSW (Farhat, 2014)} is like \textbf{optimal sensor placement}: sampling a sparse subset of elements to compute reduced matrices in microseconds.''\\

\begin{slidebox}{Page 9 | Slide 6: Geometry Configurations \hfill [04:30 -- 05:25 | 55s]}
\textit{[Point to schematic, highlighting notch depth $r$ and axial position $x_c$.]}\\
``We consider a 2D clamped cantilever beam with symmetric smooth notches parameterized by $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (r, x_c)^T \in \mathcal{D}$ under transient tip loading. Notice the core challenge: varying $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ directly deforms the physical domain $\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})$, creating incompatible function spaces across different configurations.''\\
\textbf{\color{cnamred}[CLICK NEXT SLIDE]}
\end{slidebox}

\begin{slidebox}{Page 10 | Slide 7: Governing Equations \& Assumptions \hfill [05:25 -- 06:10 | 45s]}
\textit{[Point to linear momentum balance equation.]}\\
``Under linear elasticity, small strains, and 2D plane stress, dynamics obey $\rho \ddot{\mathbf{u}} - \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} = \mathbf{b}$ in $\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})$ with Hookean elasticity, clamped Dirichlet root, and dynamic traction at the tip.''\\
\textbf{\color{cnamred}[CLICK NEXT SLIDE]}
\end{slidebox}

\begin{slidebox}{Page 11 | Slide 8: Classical Weak Form on $\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})$ \hfill [06:10 -- 06:55 | 45s]}
\textit{[Emphasize integral subscript $\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})$.]}\\
``Multiplying by test functions $\mathbf{v}$ yields the standard weak form. But because the integration domain depends on $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, snapshots would live on differing grids, preventing global basis extraction.''\\
\textbf{\color{cnamred}[CLICK NEXT SLIDE]}
\end{slidebox}

\begin{slidebox}{Page 12 | Slide 9: Pullback Mapping to Reference Configuration \hfill [06:55 -- 08:00 | 1m 05s]}
\textit{[Trace mapping arrow $\boldsymbol{\Psi}: \hat{\Omega} \to \Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})$.]}\\
``To resolve this, we introduce a \textbf{diffeomorphic pullback mapping} $\boldsymbol{\Psi}$. All equations are mapped onto an invariant reference rectangle $\hat{\Omega}$. With transformation Jacobian $\mathbf{F} = \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} \boldsymbol{\Psi}$ and determinant $J$, all gradients and measures are evaluated on $\hat{\Omega}$.''\\
\textbf{\color{cnamred}[CLICK NEXT SLIDE]}
\end{slidebox}

\begin{slidebox}{Page 13 | Slide 10: Pulled-Back Weak Form on $\hat{\Omega}$ \hfill [08:00 -- 09:00 | 1m 00s]}
\textit{[Point to pulled-back tensor $\hat{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}};\boldsymbol{\mu})$.]}\\
``On $\hat{\Omega}$, geometry variations are completely transferred from the domain boundary into the algebraic coefficients of the differential operator. All states now belong to a fixed Hilbert space $H^1(\hat{\Omega})$, enabling global model reduction.''\\
\textbf{\color{cnamred}[CLICK NEXT SLIDE]}
\end{slidebox}

\section*{Chapter 3: Discretization: Isogeometric Analysis (Pages 14 -- 25)}

\begin{slidebox}{Pages 14--18 | IGA Fundamentals (Slides 11--14) \hfill [09:00 -- 11:35 | 2m 35s]}
``We turn to Chapter 3: Isogeometric Analysis. Classical FEA replaces CAD curves with faceted segments, causing discretization errors and remeshing bottlenecks. IGA uses CAD B-Splines and NURBS directly as shape functions. Defined recursively via Cox-de Boor over knot vector $\Xi$ and control points $\mathbf{P}_i$, they ensure partition of unity, non-negativity, and compact support.''\\

\begin{slidebox}{Pages 20--25 | Bivariate Splines, $k$-Refinement \& Isoparametric (Slides 16--21) \hfill [12:35 -- 16:15 | 3m 40s]}

Furthermore, \textbf{$k$-refinement} provides $C^{p-1}$ continuity, eliminating inter-element stress jumps. Following the isoparametric concept, the dynamic displacement $\mathbf{u}_h$ is approximated using these exact functions.''\\

\section*{Chapter 4: Simulation: Full Order Model (Pages 26 -- 32)}

\begin{slidebox}{Pages 26--32 | FOM Dynamic Solver, Stress \& Verification (Slides 22--27) \hfill [16:15 -- 20:30 | 4m 15s]}
``Chapter 4 covers the Full Order Model: $\mathbf{M}\ddot{\mathbf{u}} + \mathbf{K}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{F}$ ($N = 6{,}564$ DOFs). We integrate using implicit \textbf{Newmark-$\beta$} ($\beta=1/4, \gamma=1/2$) at $\Delta t = 0.05$ s with zero artificial damping. Von Mises stress concentrates smoothly at the notch root without post-processing artifacts.\\
\textbf{Validation}: solving on reference domain $\hat{\Omega}$ matches the physical domain solver within $< 10^{-12}$ (machine precision). An $h$-convergence study confirms optimal theoretical convergence rates $O(h^p)$.''\\

\section*{Chapter 5: Reduced Order Modeling \& Hyper-Reduction (Pages 33 -- 41)}

\begin{slidebox}{Pages 33--39 | Why ROM, POD Basis \& ECSW Formulation (Slides 28--33) \hfill [20:30 -- 24:55 | 4m 25s]}
``Chapter 5: FOM takes 142 ms—far too slow for our 60 FPS ($<1.5$ ms) browser budget. In the offline phase, we collect snapshots across $\mathcal{D}$ and apply SVD. Just $r = 10$ POD modes capture $>99.99\%$ energy. However, standard Galerkin projection $\mathbf{K}_r = \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \mathbf{K}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ still integrates 3,200 cells, running at 154 ms!\\
To break this, we deploy \textbf{ECSW Hyper-reduction}: selecting just \textbf{28 active elements} via NNLS. Non-negative weights strictly guarantee symmetry, positive-definiteness, and energy stability.''\\
