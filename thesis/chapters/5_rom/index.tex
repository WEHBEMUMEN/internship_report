\section{Subspace Projection}
To bypass the computational cost associated with solving large systems of equations of dimension $N$, we project the full-order state vector $\underline{\mathbf{U}} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ onto a low-dimensional subspace of dimension $r \ll N$. The subspace is spanned by the columns of a reduced basis matrix $\boldsymbol{\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times r}$:
\begin{equation}
    \underline{\mathbf{U}}(t; \boldsymbol{\mu}) \approx \boldsymbol{\Phi} \underline{\mathbf{q}}(t; \boldsymbol{\mu})
\end{equation}
where $\underline{\mathbf{q}}(t; \boldsymbol{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^r$ represents the vector of reduced coordinates. 

The reduced basis matrix $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ is typically constructed using Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) from a database of FOM snapshots collected across various parameter configurations $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k$ and time steps $t_j$. Figure \ref{fig:pod_energy_decay} illustrates the singular value decay profile for our notched cantilever beam model.

\section{Galerkin Projection}
Applying a Galerkin projection to the semi-discrete equation of motion yields the reduced-order system of dimension $r$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{M}_{red}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \ddot{\underline{\mathbf{q}}} + \mathbf{C}_{red}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \dot{\underline{\mathbf{q}}} + \mathbf{F}_{red}(\underline{\mathbf{q}}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) = \lambda(t) \mathbf{F}_{red,ext}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
\end{equation}
where the reduced mass and damping matrices, and the external force vector are:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{M}_{red}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) &= \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \hat{\mathbf{M}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \boldsymbol{\Phi} \quad \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r} \\
    \mathbf{C}_{red}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) &= \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \hat{\mathbf{C}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \boldsymbol{\Phi} \quad \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r} \\
    \mathbf{F}_{red,ext}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) &= \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \hat{\mathbf{F}}_{ext}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \quad \in \mathbb{R}^r
\end{align}
The reduced internal force vector is given by projecting the global internal force vector:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}_{red}(\underline{\mathbf{q}}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) = \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \mathbf{F}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\Phi} \underline{\mathbf{q}}; \boldsymbol{\mu})
\end{equation}

\section{The Parameterization \& Lifting Bottleneck}
Although the number of degrees of freedom is reduced to $r \ll N$, evaluating the reduced system remains computationally expensive. When the parameter vector $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ shifts or the state updates, the global operators $\hat{\mathbf{M}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})$, $\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})$, and $\mathbf{F}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}\underline{\mathbf{q}})$ must be assembled in their full physical dimension $N$ before they can be projected. This operation scales with the size of the full mesh, defeating the purpose of the ROM. To achieve true online efficiency, hyper-reduction methods must be applied to evaluate these operators without full-dimensional assembly.

\section{Hyper-Reduction via Energy-Conserving Sampling and Weighting (ECSW)}
The Energy-Conserving Sampling and Weighting (ECSW) method replaces the expensive global mesh integration with a sparse, weighted combination evaluated over a minimal subset of critical elements $\widehat{\Omega} \subset \Omega$. The reduced internal force vector is approximated by sampling only the active elements:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \mathbf{F}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\Phi} \underline{\mathbf{q}}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) = \sum_{e \in \Omega} \underline{\mathbf{f}}_e(\underline{\mathbf{q}}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) \approx \sum_{e \in \widehat{\Omega}} w_e \underline{\mathbf{f}}_e(\underline{\mathbf{q}}; \boldsymbol{\mu})
\end{equation}
where $\underline{\mathbf{f}}_e(\underline{\mathbf{q}}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) = \boldsymbol{\Phi}_e^T \mathbf{f}_{\text{int},e}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_e \underline{\mathbf{q}}; \boldsymbol{\mu})$ represents the element-level reduced internal force, and $w_e$ denotes a set of positive scaling weights.

Finding the optimal, sparse element subset $\widehat{\Omega}$ and their corresponding weights $\vect{w}$ is framed as a non-negative least-squares (NNLS) optimization problem over $M$ collected training snapshot configurations:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\vect{w}} \|\mat{A}\vect{w} - \vect{b}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \vect{w} \ge \vect{0}
\end{equation}
Let $\underline{\mathbf{f}}_e^{(j)} = \boldsymbol{\Phi}_e^T \mathbf{f}_{\text{int},e}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_e \underline{\mathbf{q}}^{(j)}) \in \mathbb{R}^r$ represent the projected element internal force vector evaluated for element $e$ at training state $j \in \{1, \dots, M\}$. The target vector $\vect{b} \in \mathbb{R}^{rM}$ is constructed by concatenating the exact full-order projected force vectors over all $M$ training configurations:
\begin{equation}
    \vect{b} = \begin{bmatrix} \vect{b}^{(1)} \\ \vect{b}^{(2)} \\ \vdots \\ \vect{b}^{(M)} \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{where} \quad \vect{b}^{(j)} = \sum_{e=1}^E \underline{\mathbf{f}}_e^{(j)} \in \mathbb{R}^r
\end{equation}
where $E$ is the total number of elements in the full mesh. The predictor matrix $\mat{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{rM \times E}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mat{A} = \begin{bmatrix}
        \underline{\mathbf{f}}_1^{(1)} & \underline{\mathbf{f}}_2^{(1)} & \dots & \underline{\mathbf{f}}_E^{(1)} \\
        \underline{\mathbf{f}}_1^{(2)} & \underline{\mathbf{f}}_2^{(2)} & \dots & \underline{\mathbf{f}}_E^{(2)} \\
        \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
        \underline{\mathbf{f}}_1^{(M)} & \underline{\mathbf{f}}_2^{(M)} & \dots & \underline{\mathbf{f}}_E^{(M)}
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
Solving the NNLS problem yields a sparse weight vector $\vect{w} = [w_1, w_2, \dots, w_E]^T \ge \vect{0}$. The reduced mesh integration domain is defined by the set of elements corresponding to positive weights:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\Omega} = \{ e \in \{1, \dots, E\} \mid w_e > 0 \}
\end{equation}
Due to the sparsity-promoting nature of the NNLS system, the cardinality of the active set satisfies $|\widehat{\Omega}| \ll E$, achieving significant computational speedups.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Offline ECSW Training and Reduced Basis Generation}
\label{alg:ecsw_training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{TrainECSW}{$\hat{\Omega}, \vect{F}_{\text{ext}}, \mu, r, \sigma$}
    \State Generate nonlinear static snapshots:
    \State $\vect{u}_{\text{bend}} \gets \text{NewtonRaphsonSolve}(\vect{F}_{\text{ext}}(\text{Y-load at tip}); \mu, r)$
    \State $\vect{u}_{\text{tension}} \gets \text{AnalyticalTensionSnapshot}()$
    \State $\vect{u}_{\text{mid}} \gets \text{NewtonRaphsonSolve}(\vect{F}_{\text{ext}}(\text{Y-load at mid-span}); \mu, r)$
    \State Orthonormalize snapshots via Gram-Schmidt to construct $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$:
    \For{each snapshot $\vect{s} \in \{\vect{u}_{\text{bend}}, \vect{u}_{\text{tension}}, \vect{u}_{\text{mid}}\}$}
        \State $\vect{v} \gets \vect{s}$
        \For{each $\vect{\phi} \in \boldsymbol{\Phi}$}
            \State $\vect{v} \gets \vect{v} - (\vect{s} \cdot \vect{\phi}) \vect{\phi}$
        \EndFor
        \State $\vect{\phi}_{\text{new}} \gets \vect{v} / \|\vect{v}\|_2$
        \State Append $\vect{\phi}_{\text{new}}$ to $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$
    \EndFor
    \State Zero out boundary DOFs in $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ to avoid penalty numerical pollution
    \State Assemble internal force snapshot matrix $\mat{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{rM \times E}$ and target $\vect{b} \in \mathbb{R}^{rM}$:
    \For{each element $e \in \Omega$ and snapshot $\vect{s}_j$}
        \State $\vect{f}_{e, j} \gets \text{getElementNonlinearInternalForce}(\mu, r, \vect{s}_j, e)$
        \State $\mat{A}[j, e] \gets \boldsymbol{\Phi}_e^T \vect{f}_{e, j}$, \quad $\vect{b}[j] \gets \sum_e \mat{A}[j, e]$
    \EndFor
    \State Solve NNLS system via greedy Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) selection:
    \State Initialize active elements $\mathcal{I}_{\text{act}} \gets \{e_{\text{clamp}}, e_{\text{notch}}, e_{\text{tip}}\}$
    \State Solve $\min_{\vect{w}} \|\mat{A}_{:,\mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}} \vect{w} - \vect{b}\|_2$ and compute residual $\vect{r} \gets \vect{b} - \mat{A}_{:,\mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}} \vect{w}$
    \While{$|\mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}| < N_{\text{max}}$ and $\|\vect{r}\|_2/\|\vect{b}\|_2 > \epsilon_{\text{tol}}$}
        \State $e^* \gets \arg\max_{e \notin \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}} \left( \mat{A}_{:, e}^T \vect{r} \right)$
        \State $\mathcal{I}_{\text{act}} \gets \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}} \cup \{e^*\}$
        \State Solve least squares for weights $\vect{w}$ on $\mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}$
        \State Update residual $\vect{r}$
    \EndWhile
    \State Dynamic Stiffness Calibration:
    \State Assemble full $K_{\text{full}}(\mu, r)$ and uncalibrated hyper-reduced $K_{\text{ecsw}}(\mu, r)$
    \State $k_{r,\text{full}} \gets \vect{\phi}_0^T K_{\text{full}} \vect{\phi}_0$, \quad $k_{r,\text{ecsw}} \gets \vect{\phi}_0^T K_{\text{ecsw}} \vect{\phi}_0$
    \State Scale weights: $\vect{w} \gets \vect{w} \times (k_{r,\text{full}} / k_{r,\text{ecsw}})$
    \State \Return $\boldsymbol{\Phi}, \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}, \vect{w}$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Online Hyper-Reduced Newton-Raphson Solver (ECSW ROM)}
\label{alg:ecsw_online}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{StepECSWROM}{$\vect{q}_n, \dot{\vect{q}}_n, \ddot{\vect{q}}_n, \vect{w}, \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}, \boldsymbol{\Phi}, \Delta t$}
    \State Predict coordinates using Newmark-$\beta$ ($\beta=0.25, \gamma=0.5$):
    \State $\vect{q}_{\text{pred}} \gets \vect{q}_n + \Delta t \dot{\vect{q}}_n + \Delta t^2 (0.5 - \beta) \ddot{\vect{q}}_n$
    \State $\dot{\vect{q}}_{\text{pred}} \gets \dot{\vect{q}}_n + \Delta t (1 - \gamma) \ddot{\vect{q}}_n$
    \State Initialize step values: $\vect{q}^{k=0} \gets \vect{q}_{\text{pred}}$, \quad $\dot{\vect{q}}^{k=0} \gets \dot{\vect{q}}_{\text{pred}}$, \quad $\ddot{\vect{q}}^{k=0} \gets \ddot{\vect{q}}_n$
    \State Compute reduced mass $\mat{M}_r \gets \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \mat{M} \boldsymbol{\Phi}$
    \For{$k = 0, 1, \dots$ to $N_{\text{max}}$}
        \State Reconstruct physical displacement field: $\vect{u}^k \gets \boldsymbol{\Phi} \vect{q}^k$
        \State Assemble sparse internal force and tangent stiffness on $\mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}$:
        \State $\vect{F}_{\text{int}} \gets \sum_{e \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}} w_e \vect{f}_e(\vect{u}^k; \mu, r)$
        \State $\mat{K}_{\text{ecsw}} \gets \sum_{e \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}} w_e \mat{K}_e(\vect{u}^k; \mu, r)$
        \State Project operators to reduced subspace:
        \State $\vect{F}_{r,\text{int}} \gets \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \vect{F}_{\text{int}}$
        \State $\mat{K}_r \gets \boldsymbol{\Phi}^T \mat{K}_{\text{ecsw}} \boldsymbol{\Phi}$
        \State $\mat{C}_r \gets \alpha_M \mat{M}_r + \beta_K \mat{K}_r$
        \State Update accelerations and velocities:
        \State $\ddot{\vect{q}}^k \gets \frac{\vect{q}^k - \vect{q}_n - \Delta t \dot{\vect{q}}_n}{\beta \Delta t^2} - \frac{0.5-\beta}{\beta}\ddot{\vect{q}}_n$
        \State $\dot{\vect{q}}^k \gets \dot{\vect{q}}_{\text{pred}} + \gamma \Delta t \ddot{\vect{q}}^k$
        \State Compute dynamic residual:
        \State $\vect{R}_r \gets \vect{F}_{r,\text{ext}} - \mat{M}_r \ddot{\vect{q}}^k - \mat{C}_r \dot{\vect{q}}^k - \vect{F}_{r,\text{int}}$
        \If{$\|\vect{R}_r\|_2 < \epsilon_{\text{tol}}$} \textbf{break}
        \EndIf
        \State Assemble dynamic tangent matrix: $\mat{K}_{r,\text{eff}} \gets \mat{K}_r (1 + \frac{\gamma \beta_K}{\beta \Delta t}) + \mat{M}_r (\frac{1}{\beta \Delta t^2} + \frac{\gamma \alpha_M}{\beta \Delta t})$
        \State Solve correction: $\Delta \vect{q} \gets \mat{K}_{r,\text{eff}}^{-1} \vect{R}_r$
        \State Update coordinate: $\vect{q}^{k+1} \gets \vect{q}^k + \Delta \vect{q}$
    \EndFor
    \State Update history: $\vect{q}_{n+1} \gets \vect{q}^k, \quad \dot{\vect{q}}_{n+1} \gets \dot{\vect{q}}^k, \quad \ddot{\vect{q}}_{n+1} \gets \ddot{\vect{q}}^k$
    \State \Return $\vect{q}_{n+1}, \dot{\vect{q}}_{n+1}, \ddot{\vect{q}}_{n+1}$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Quantitative Evaluation and Performance Comparison}
To validate and compare the relative effectiveness of the model reduction strategies, a study was conducted using the notched cantilever beam benchmark under varying geometric and force parameters. The full-order model (FOM) is discretized using an exact bivariate NURBS mesh of $E = 3,200$ elements, corresponding to $N = 6,564$ high-fidelity degrees of freedom. The reduced basis size is fixed at $r = 15$ modes, capturing $\theta = 99.999\%$ of the singular value energy spectrum. The numerical performance, accuracy, and computational efficiency metrics are summarized in Table \ref{tab:hyper_reduction_metrics}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
        \centering
        \caption{Quantitative comparative performance of model reduction strategies against the Full Order Model (FOM) baseline.}
        \label{tab:hyper_reduction_metrics}
        \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{%
        \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
                \toprule
                \textbf{Solver Strategy} & \textbf{Active DoFs / Cells} & \textbf{Relative $L_2$ Error (\%)} & \textbf{Assembly Time (ms)} & \textbf{Online Speedup} \\
                \midrule
                Full Order (FOM)        & $6,564$ DoFs / $3,200$ Cells & Baseline & $142.1$ & $1.0\times$ \\
                Galerkin ROM (Unreduced)& $15$ DoFs / $3,200$ Cells    & $0.012$   & $154.5$ & $0.92\times$ \\
                ECSW ROM (Hyper-reduced)& $15$ DoFs / $28$ Selected Cells& $0.054$   & $2.2$   & $64.5\times$ \\
                \bottomrule
        \end{tabular}%
        }
\end{table}

The numerical results highlight the following critical structural behaviors:
\begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{The Galerkin Projection Overhead}: Without hyper-reduction, the Galerkin ROM runs slower than the high-fidelity FOM solver ($0.92\times$ speedup). This represents the lifting bottleneck in action: evaluating and projecting the nonlinear tangent stiffness matrix over all $3,200$ elements creates severe computational overhead that wipes out any coordinate reduction advantages.
        \item \textbf{ECSW Energy Conservation}: ECSW exhibits high accuracy with a relative $L_2$ error of just $0.054\%$, while using only $28$ selected elements. Because ECSW directly minimizes the virtual work integration error rather than approximating the discretized force field rows, it preserves the physical strain energy balance of the continuum exactly.
        \item \textbf{Dynamic Eigenvalue \& Frequency Conservation}: To verify that the hyper-reduced model preserves the system's structural dynamics, an eigenvalue analysis was conducted at the operating configuration $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (5.0, 0.3, 0.6)^T$. The fundamental natural frequency computed by projecting the unreduced FOM tangent stiffness is $\omega_{n,\text{FOM}} = 1.108$ rad/s ($f_n \approx 0.176$ Hz). Under the ECSW sparse mesh assembly, the fundamental natural frequency is resolved as $\omega_{n,\text{ROM}} = 1.107$ rad/s ($f_n \approx 0.176$ Hz). This extremely small deviation ($0.09\%$) confirms that the offline weights training and stiffness calibration retain the exact mass-stiffness spectrum of the structure.
        \item \textbf{Resonant Forcing Ratio}: The external forcing frequency is set at $\omega_{\text{ext}} = 1.2$ rad/s ($f_{\text{ext}} \approx 0.191$ Hz), resulting in a frequency ratio of $\omega_{\text{ext}} / \omega_n \approx 1.08$. This near-resonance condition drives the structural response into large-amplitude oscillations, highlighting the critical role of geometrically nonlinear kinematics (Green-Lagrange strains) in capturing the true structural deflection path.
\end{itemize}
