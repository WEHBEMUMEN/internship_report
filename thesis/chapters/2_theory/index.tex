\section{Geometry Configurations}
In structural mechanics and shape optimization, we analyze physical domains under geometric variations. Let $\Omega_0 \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) define the initial physical coordinate configuration, and let $\Omega(t; \boldsymbol{\mu})$ define the deformed physical configuration at time $t$, parameterized by a vector of design variables $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathcal{D} \subset \mathbb{R}^P$.

To systematically analyze the structural response under geometric parameter variations, we distinguish between two physical configurations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Initial Physical Configuration} $\Omega_0$: The spatial domain of the structure in its undeformed state at $t = 0$.
    \item \textbf{Deformed Physical Configuration} $\Omega(t; \boldsymbol{\mu})$: The spatial domain of the structure at time $t$ under external loads, subject to the geometric parameter vector $\boldsymbol{\mu}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Parameterized Framework}
We consider a 2D cantilever beam under geometric parameter variations. The geometric parameters are grouped into a vector:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mu} = (r, \mu, \sigma)^T \in \mathcal{D} \subset \mathbb{R}^3
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $r$ represents the radius of the symmetric circular notches cut out from the top and bottom boundaries.
    \item $\mu$ denotes the spatial position (distance from the clamped boundary on the left) of the notch center along the longitudinal axis of the beam.
    \item $\sigma$ represents the external loading parameters and stress multipliers.
\end{itemize}
The displacement field of the structure is denoted by $\underline{u}(\vect{x}, t; \boldsymbol{\mu})$ for $\vect{x} \in \Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})$, and the stress state is represented by the Cauchy stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$.

\section{Governing Equations \& Kinematics}
Assuming small strains and linear elastic isotropic material behavior, the strong form governing the transient response of the structural continuum on the parameterized physical domain $\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})$ over the time interval $(0, T]$ is given by the balance of linear momentum:
\begin{equation}
    \rho \ddot{\underline{u}} - \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\underline{u}) = \underline{f}_b \quad \text{in } \Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \times (0, T]
\end{equation}
subject to the parameterized Dirichlet boundary conditions on $\Gamma_D(\boldsymbol{\mu})$ (fixed support at the left end) and Neumann boundary conditions on $\Gamma_N(\boldsymbol{\mu})$ (external loads applied to the boundaries):
\begin{align}
    \underline{u} &= \underline{0} \quad \text{on } \Gamma_D(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \times (0, T] \\
    \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\underline{u}) \cdot \underline{n} &= \underline{h}_N \quad \text{on } \Gamma_N(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \times (0, T]
\end{align}
where $\rho$ is the material density, $\underline{f}_b$ is the body force vector, and $\underline{n}$ is the outward unit normal vector. The kinematic relation defines the linearized strain tensor $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\underline{u})$ as:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\underline{u}) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\underline{u} + (\nabla\underline{u})^T\right)
\end{equation}
For an isotropic material, the Cauchy stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ is related to the strain tensor via Hooke's law:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\underline{u}) = \mat{D} : \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\underline{u})
\end{equation}
where $\mat{D}$ is the fourth-order elasticity tensor (which under plane stress assumptions reduces to a $3 \times 3$ constitutive matrix).

\section{Weak Formulation at the Continuum Level}
To derive the variational statement, we multiply the momentum balance equation by a test function $\delta\underline{u}$ belonging to the space of kinematically admissible variations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{V}_0(\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})) = \{ \underline{v} \in [H^1(\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu}))]^2 \mid \underline{v} = \underline{0} \text{ on } \Gamma_D(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \}
\end{equation}
Integrating by parts over the parameterized spatial domain $\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})$ yields the weak form:
\begin{equation}
    m(\ddot{\underline{u}}, \delta\underline{u}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) + k(\underline{u}, \delta\underline{u}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) = f(\delta\underline{u}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) \quad \forall \delta\underline{u} \in \mathcal{V}_0(\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
\end{equation}
where the bilinear mass and stiffness forms, and the linear load form are defined respectively as:
\begin{align}
    m(\ddot{\underline{u}}, \delta\underline{u}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) &= \int_{\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})} \rho \ddot{\underline{u}} \cdot \delta\underline{u} \, d\Omega \\
    k(\underline{u}, \delta\underline{u}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) &= \int_{\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})} \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\underline{u}) : \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\delta\underline{u}) \, d\Omega \\
    f(\delta\underline{u}; \boldsymbol{\mu}) &= \int_{\Omega(\boldsymbol{\mu})} \underline{f}_b \cdot \delta\underline{u} \, d\Omega + \int_{\Gamma_N(\boldsymbol{\mu})} \underline{h}_N \cdot \delta\underline{u} \, d\Gamma
\end{align}
