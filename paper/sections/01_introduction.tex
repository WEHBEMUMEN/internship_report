Modern structural engineering and cyber-physical systems increasingly demand digital twins capable of predicting transient mechanical behavior under real-time operational constraints \cite{hughes2005, Benner2015}. Traditional structural monitoring relies either on sparse sensor arrays or computationally expensive Finite Element (FE) simulations that cannot run synchronously with physical operations.

Isogeometric Analysis (IGA), pioneered by Hughes et al. \cite{hughes2005}, unifies Computer-Aided Design (CAD) and Computer-Aided Engineering (CAE) by utilizing CAD basis functions—such as B-splines and Non-Uniform Rational B-Splines (NURBS)—directly for field approximations. Beyond geometric exactness, IGA exhibits superior per-degree-of-freedom accuracy and elevated spectrum fidelity compared to standard $C^0$-continuous Finite Element Methods (FEM) \cite{cottrell2009}.

However, despite these advantages, high-fidelity IGA models for large-scale transient structural dynamics remain computationally intensive. Evaluating high-dimensional stiffness and mass matrices at each time step prevents real-time interactive execution. Reduced Order Modeling (ROM), specifically Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) coupled with Galerkin projection, offers an effective framework for projecting high-dimensional state vectors onto low-dimensional subspaces \cite{Benner2015}.

In this paper, we propose an integrated framework bridging NURBS-based IGA with POD-Galerkin reduction for real-time elastodynamic simulation. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Formulation of a unified IGA-POD Galerkin projection solver for linear and nonlinearly constrained elastodynamics.
    \item Spectrum preservation analysis comparing high-order IGA basis continuity ($C^{p-1}$) against $C^0$ FE discretizations under projection-based model reduction.
    \item Deployment of an online interactive 3D web-based digital twin visualizer achieving frame rates exceeding 24 FPS with sub-millisecond solver step evaluations.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section~\ref{sec:methodology} reviews the continuum mechanics formulation and IGA fundamentals. Section~\ref{sec:implementation} outlines the offline/online POD-Galerkin reduction procedure. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents numerical benchmarks, validation metrics, and performance speedup comparisons. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.
