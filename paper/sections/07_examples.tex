\subsection{2D Benchmark: Parameterized Notched Structural Component}
\label{subsec:2d_example}

\subsubsection{Problem Setup and Reference Pullback}
To evaluate the proposed IGA-pROM-ECSW methodology under combined geometric variations and transient elastodynamics, we first consider a parameterized 2D structural plate featuring a variable circular notch. The physical domain $\Omega(\bm{\mu})$ is clamped along the left edge ($x = 0$) and subjected to a dynamic sinusoidal vertical traction $\bar{\bm{t}}(t) = [0, \, F_0 \sin(\omega t)]^\top$ on the top boundary over $t \in [0, T]$ with $T = 5.6\,\text{s}$ ($\Delta t = 0.08\,\text{s}$). The material is linear elastic with Young's modulus $E = 210\,\text{GPa}$, Poisson's ratio $\nu = 0.30$, and mass density $\rho = 7850\,\text{kg/m}^3$.

The geometric parameter vector $\bm{\mu} = (x_c, r_{\text{notch}})^\top$ controls the center position $x_c \in [0.5, 4.5]\,\text{m}$ and radius $r_{\text{notch}} \in [0.1, 0.45]\,\text{m}$ of the geometric notch within the admissible domain $\mathcal{P}_{\text{admissible}}$. Using the pullback mapping $\bm{\Phi}(\bm{\xi}; \bm{\mu})$, the varying physical configurations are mapped onto the invariant reference rectangle $\hat{\Omega} = [0, L] \times [0, H]$. The domain is discretized using bi-quadratic B-splines ($p = q = 2$) with $C^1$ inter-element continuity across all internal knot spans, producing $E = 3,200$ elements and $N_{\text{dof}} = 6,888$ displacement degrees of freedom for the Full Order Model (FOM).

\subsubsection{Hyper-Reduction and Performance Comparison}
Training snapshots are generated by running FOM time integrations across a structured $5 \times 4$ parameter grid over $\mathcal{P}_{\text{admissible}}$. Applying POD to the snapshot matrix yields steep singular value decay: $r = 15$ modes capture over $99.999\%$ of the total cumulative energy. Next, ECSW training is executed via NNLS optimization with coercivity pre-seeding ($\mathcal{E}_{\text{seed}} = \mathcal{E}_{\text{clamp}} \cup \mathcal{E}_{\text{notch}}$). Setting a residual tolerance of $\varepsilon_{\text{tol}} = 1 \times 10^{-4}$ selects an active subset of merely $E^* = 28$ elements out of $3,200$ (an element sparsity reduction of $99.12\%$).

To disentangle the computational gain of dimensionality reduction from that of sparse integration, we perform a controlled three-solver comparison on a representative unseen parameter instance $\bm{\mu}^*$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full Order Model (FOM):} Full Newmark implicit solve over all $E = 3,200$ elements and $N_{\text{dof}} = 6,888$ DOFs.
    \item \textbf{Galerkin ROM (Unreduced Assembly):} Rank-$r = 15$ POD projection, but assembling the operator over all $E = 3,200$ elements.
    \item \textbf{ECSW Hyper-reduced ROM:} Rank-$r = 15$ POD projection with assembly restricted strictly to the $E^* = 28$ selected elements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{3D Benchmark: Parameterized Cantilever Structural Component}
\label{subsec:3d_example}

\subsubsection{Discretization and Dynamic Impulse Loading}
To demonstrate scalability to three-dimensional elastodynamics, we evaluate a 3D structural cantilever beam subjected to dynamic tip impulse excitation. The component has length $L = 2.0\,\text{m}$ and square cross section $0.2 \times 0.2\,\text{m}$, with material constants $E = 210\,\text{GPa}$, $\nu = 0.30$, and $\rho = 7850\,\text{kg/m}^3$. 

The volume is discretized using trivariate cubic B-spline elements ($p = q = s = 3$) with $C^2$ inter-element continuity across all internal knot spans. This high continuity ensures accurate representation of high-frequency flexural and torsional wave packets without spurious numerical damping, resulting in $N_{\text{dof}} = 12,450$ displacement DOFs. The cantilever is clamped at the root ($x = 0$) and subjected to a transient decaying harmonic tip load $F_z(t) = F_0 e^{-\alpha t} \sin(\omega t)$.

\subsubsection{Singular Value Spectrum and Mode Convergence}
The singular value decay spectrum $\sigma_i$ of the offline snapshot matrix and the cumulative energy ratio $\mathcal{E}(r)$ are plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:singular_values}. The spectrum exhibits rapid exponential decay, dropping by over four orders of magnitude within the first 15 modes. By selecting $r = 12$ reduced modes, the captured cumulative energy ratio reaches $\mathcal{E}(r) > 99.999\%$.
